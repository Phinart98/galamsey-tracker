# Build galamsey tracking on radar, not promises

An open, autonomous galamsey tracker for Ghana in late 2026 should be built on **Sentinel-1 C-band radar as the primary detector**. Use analysis-ready terrain-corrected backscatter (OPERA RTC-S1, with Planetary Computer and ASF HyP3 as interchangeable sources) and pair it with **Sentinel-2 L2A optical masked with Cloud Score+** for confirmation and river turbidity. **OPERA DIST-ALERT** (the farmland-covering component now inside the Global Nature Watch integrated alerts) works as an independent off-the-shelf comparator, read as COG files rather than through the GFW query API. NISAR L-band is the most promising addition, but its products are still provisional and due to be reprocessed. Radar comes first because cloud, not data latency, sets the pace over southwestern Ghana. Radar also matters because most galamsey happens in cocoa farms, farmland and river beds, where the forest-only alert systems are blind. Everything recommended here is free, and most of it is CC-BY compatible. The exceptions that matter are Planet NICFI, which cannot be redistributed, Google Earth Engine's ambiguous publishing terms, and SmallMinesDS's share-alike licence. "Real time" honestly means an alert confirmed **two to three weeks** after the ground is disturbed, and a monthly-to-quarterly optical picture in the rainy seasons. The last attempt failed on fragile dependencies, so the architecture matters as much as the data. The pipeline should read static COGs through STAC on GitHub Actions and treat every hosted API as optional.

## Two radar satellites now restore six-day coverage, and that is the backbone

Sentinel-1 has had a turbulent 2026. Sentinel-1D launched on 4 November 2025 ([ESA](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Copernicus/Sentinel-1D_reaches_orbit_on_Ariane_6)) and became fully operational on **1 May 2026** ([Military Embedded Systems](https://militaryembedded.com/comms/satellites/radar-satellite-becomes-operational-for-european-earth-observation-mission)). Sentinel-1A ended operations on **29–30 June 2026** after twelve years ([ESA](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Copernicus/Sentinel-1/Time_to_say_goodbye_to_Sentinel-1A); [EC DEFIS](https://defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu/sentinel-1a-concludes-its-mission-leaving-lasting-legacy-copernicus-2026-06-30_en)). S1C and S1D now fly 180 degrees apart. That gives a **12-day repeat per satellite and 6 days for the constellation** ([ESA FRINGE 2026](https://fringe2026.esa.int/iframe-agenda/files/ID_537.pdf)), with IW mode and a fixed polarisation scheme over land to support time series ([Sentinel-1 Observation Scenario](https://sentinels.copernicus.eu/copernicus/sentinel-1/observation-scenario)). Two practical consequences follow. First, any code that filters on platform `S1A` stops receiving data after June 2026, so filtering has to be platform-agnostic. Second, nobody has yet confirmed that Ghana gets the full 6-day, two-satellite repeat rather than the 12-day single-satellite coverage West Africa lived with during the S1B gap. That has to be counted, not assumed.

Sentinel-1 needs to be terrain-flattened. The Ashanti and Western hills distort raw sigma0. Work on Ghanaian mining found terrain-normalised gamma0 outperforms sigma0 ([Mensah thesis](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/id/eprint/21766/1/Isaac%20Obour%20Mensah%20Thesis.pdf)). **OPERA RTC-S1** delivers exactly that: 30 m gamma0 RTC in VV+VH, near-global, in Earth Engine from April 2016 with daily updates ([GEE](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/OPERA_RTC_L2_V1_S1)), and also on the AWS Open Data registry ([AWS](https://registry.opendata.aws/nasa-operal2rtc-s1v1/)). Planetary Computer's `sentinel-1-rtc` was still updating in October 2026 ([PC Explorer](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/explore?d=sentinel-1-rtc)). It had real gaps this year, though: missing items in February and an ingest stoppage from 17 April ([Issue #472](https://github.com/microsoft/PlanetaryComputer/issues/472); [issues list](https://github.com/microsoft/PlanetaryComputer/issues)). That makes it a good second source but a poor sole source. **ASF HyP3** is the on-demand fallback. It gives 8,000 free credits a month, an RTC job costs 5 credits at 30 m, 15 at 20 m or 60 at 10 m, and outputs are kept for only 14 days, so they must be copied to the project's own storage ([HyP3 credits](https://hyp3-docs.asf.alaska.edu/using/credits/); [HyP3 docs](https://hyp3-docs.asf.alaska.edu/)). Southern Ghana needs only a handful of frames per pass, so HyP3 can rebuild a missed month comfortably within quota.

The method is where the previous attempt was weakest. The anchor study is Forkuor, Ullmann and Griesbeck (2020). They used annual Sentinel-1 series along southwestern rivers and found the **annual minimum backscatter** in VV and VH more sensitive than the mean or maximum. A **+1.65 dB** increase flagged 102, 60 and 33 km² of new mining in successive year pairs ([Würzburg OPUS](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-wuerzburg.de/files/20320/remotesensing-12-00911.pdf)). A Cameroon follow-up used +1.55 dB ([MUBAS](https://publications.mubas.ac.mw/publication/assessing-impacts-of-small-scale-mining-activities-in-eastern-cameroon-recent-contributions-from-sentinel-1-time-series-2017-2021)). Heavy rain adds noise that calls for temporal smoothing ([Glasgow eprints](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/352994)). Two caveats are critical. The full paper's orbit choice, threshold derivation and accuracy figures could not be retrieved, and the method is annual, not near-real-time. Any rolling-window version is therefore a new, unvalidated detector until it is scored against labelled data. The change is also likely mixed in sign: rough spoil should raise the backscatter floor, while open-water ponds go dark. That mechanism comes from SAR physics, not from a Ghana measurement, so a one-sided threshold risks missing ponds. The open-source **dist-s1** workflow, which produces OPERA DIST-ALERT-S1 from the RTC time series, is a ready-made comparator that can run on a custom AOI ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/dist-s1/2.0.18/)). Earthdata lists the product itself as near-global on 30 m MGRS tiles ([Earthdata DIST-ALERT-S1](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/asf-opera-l3-dist-alert-s1-v1-1)).

## Cloud, not latency, sets the optical clock over the southwest

The optical fleet is temporarily generous. Sentinel-2C took over the operational slot from 2A, and ESA kept **2A flying in an extension campaign until 31 December 2026** ([Sentinels Copernicus](https://sentinels.copernicus.eu/-/sentinel-2a-extension-campaign-prolonged-until-the-end-of-2026)). 2A sits a day off 2C and two days off 2B on the same orbit, which shortens revisit below the nominal five days ([European Spaceflight](https://europeanspaceflight.com/sentinel-2a-given-one-year-exceptional-and-temporary-extension/)). Nothing has been announced for 2027, so plan for a five-day revisit from two satellites. Data latency is small: older figures put L1C at 2–9 hours after sensing, with L2A a few hours behind ([Sentinel Hub](https://www.sentinel-hub.com/newsletters/20190123/)). No current official timeliness spec was found. **HLS v2** harmonises Landsat 8/9 and Sentinel-2 to 30 m with about **1.7–3 days** latency ([NASA Earthdata](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/projects/nsite/solutions/hls)). A 6-hour low-latency HLS is still in development ([Earthdata](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/about/nasa-support-snwg/solutions/low-latency-hls-products)). Landsat Next will not fly before about 2031 ([MAAT report](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/esd/earth-science-division/key-documents/landsat_next_MAAT_report_public.pdf)).

None of that matters much once clouds arrive. Cloud cover over the Gulf of Guinea reaches **75–80%** in some seasons ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/328989895_Use_of_Sentinel-l_and_Sentinel-2_for_Monitoring_Illegal_Fishing_Off_Ghana)). Cocoa mapping in Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire needed **six-month windows** to collect ten usable scenes per tile ([arXiv 2206.06119](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.06119)). No published count of clear observations per month exists for Ashanti, Western or Eastern regions, and that is the first number the spike should produce. The best free per-pixel mask is **Cloud Score+**: 10 m, CC-BY-4.0, current to the present day, with a recommended `cs_cdf` threshold of 0.60 ([GEE](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/GOOGLE_CLOUD_SCORE_PLUS_V1_S2_HARMONIZED)). One catch is that it is published in Earth Engine, so a pipeline built outside GEE needs a substitute such as the L2A scene classification layer, or a small GEE pull.

Optical still does work radar cannot. Gallwey et al. mapped mining across about 6 Mha of southern Ghana with a Sentinel-2 CNN at **under 8% omission and commission error** ([Exeter ORE](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/122294)). Barenblitt et al. measured about **47,000 ha** converted to mining in the southwest between 2005 and 2019 ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210016961)). Optical is also the only route to river turbidity. In-situ values on the Pra reach **2,010–2,745 NTU** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36582700)), against a 5 NTU drinking standard ([3news](https://3news.com/ghana-water-company-makes-gains-on-galamsey-ban/)). Yet no Ghana-calibrated satellite turbidity model exists. Copernicus Lake Water Quality v2 covers only lakes of roughly 50 ha or more ([DE Africa](https://docs.digitalearthafrica.org/en/latest/data_specs/CGLM_Lake_Water_Quality_specs.html)), which rules out rivers. A 2026 Ghana study found Sentinel-2 better for large water bodies but Landsat better at narrow, connected mining-impacted channels ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2950509726000316)). The defensible product is a relative index, such as an NDTI anomaly against a pre-boom baseline, restricted to wide reaches. Absolute NTU would saturate at these concentrations and claim a precision the data cannot support.

## Forest alerts miss the farms, and free high-resolution imagery is gone

The ready-made alert landscape changed in January 2026. GFW, rebranded **Global Nature Watch** in September 2026 ([WRI](https://www.wri.org/news/release-global-forest-watch-becomes-global-nature-watch-expanding-cover-more-land-ecosystems)), added DIST-ALERT and renamed its layer "integrated disturbance alerts". The layer now covers all vegetation, including crops, but **outside forest only DIST-ALERT contributes**, and DIST-ALERT has no low-confidence class ([GNW blog](https://globalnaturewatch.org/blog/data-and-tools/integrated-deforestation-alerts/)). RADD's 10 m cloud-penetrating radar alerts are CC BY 4.0 but limited to primary humid tropical forest ([GEE community catalog](https://gee-community-catalog.org/projects/radd/); [WUR](https://www.wur.nl/en/research/products-services/radd-forest-disturbance-alert)), which in Ghana mostly means forest reserves. That is a coverage gap at the core of the problem. Galamsey concentrates in cocoa, farmland and river beds, and in-channel dredging shows up as a sediment change that no vegetation alert is designed to catch. The project's own record shows a second problem: the GFW `/query/json` endpoint has returned 500s on every dataset version since v20260517, and no public changelog explains why. The Data API paths are unchanged ([ReDoc](https://data-api.globalforestwatch.org/)), but GFW Pro and its API retire on 31 December 2026 ([GFW Pro](https://pro-news.globalforestwatch.org/news-events/gfw-pro-is-consolidating-into-global-nature-watch)), which is a warning about platform churn. The robust route is to skip the query API and read **OPERA DIST-ALERT-HLS** COGs directly. They map disturbance every 2–4 days at 30 m and are distributed through LP DAAC and the AWS Open Data registry ([Earthdata](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/opera-land-surface-disturbance-alert-version-1-data-product-released); [AWS](https://registry.opendata.aws/nasa-snwg-opera/)).

High-resolution imagery is no longer a free option. NICFI's first phase ended on 23 January 2025 ([NICFI](https://www.nicfi.no/2025/01/28/nicfi-satellite-data-program-enters-new-phase/)), and Norway **cancelled the follow-on procurement** in September 2025 with no timeline for a replacement ([NICFI](https://www.nicfi.no/2025/09/09/norway-cancels-procurement-for-tropical-forest-satellite-data-program/)). The Earth Engine collection still lists mosaics to September 2026, but it is gated, non-commercial and carries "usage, reproduction, and distribution restrictions" ([GEE NICFI](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/projects_planet-nicfi_assets_basemaps_africa)). An open CC-BY tracker can therefore use NICFI only as private visual verification, if at all. That matters beyond this project: CERSGIS, the main Ghanaian institutional mapper, builds on NICFI monthly mosaics ([AGU 2023](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023AGUFMSY51A0578A/abstract)), and its portal gives no method, cadence or downloads ([SERVIR](https://servir.icrisat.org/artisanal-mining-galamsey-monitoring/)). GalamseyWatch.org claims real-time satellite monitoring but names no operator, source or licence ([galamseywatch.org](https://galamseywatch.org/)). The gap this project fills is a documented, downloadable, regularly updated public map that also covers farmland. Sentinel-2's 10 m is the finest free, open, regular optical source available.

## NISAR adds canopy-piercing radar, with provisional strings attached

NISAR is the one genuinely new source of 2026. NASA's L-band products are open and global through ASF. Calibrated **PROVISIONAL** GCOV and GSLC products have been public since 20 July 2026, for acquisitions from 17 June onward. Latency is about **36–72 hours**, and validated reprocessed products are due in Q4 2026, superseding earlier versions ([ASF](https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/availability-overview/)). There is also a permanent gap from 27 July to 10 August 2026 (same source). GCOV gives normalised backscatter per polarisation ([ARSET 2026](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2026-07/arset-2026-nisar-part2.pdf)). L-band's strength here is physical: it penetrates canopy, so the loss of cocoa or forest cover to a bare pit should show up strongly in HV even in the wet season. The costs are real for a solo developer. The format is HDF5 rather than COG, there is no GEE listing, and a full re-ingest is guaranteed once validated products land. S-band data sits only on ISRO's Bhoonidhi portal under unverified terms ([ISRO](https://www.isro.gov.in/ISRO_EN/NISARS_Band_SAR_Data_Products_Release.html)). The older L-band archive, ALOS-2 PALSAR-2 ScanSAR at 25 m in GEE, is free with attribution ([GEE](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/JAXA_ALOS_PALSAR-2_Level2_2_ScanSAR)). Its cadence is likely too sparse for alerts, but it is a clean historical baseline. ALOS-4 data is sold commercially through Tenchijin ([Synspective](https://synspective.com/data/alos-4-data/)).

For validation, the open labelled data is good. **SmallMinesDS** provides 4,270 co-registered patches of Sentinel-2, Sentinel-1 RTC and DEM data with mine masks across five southwestern districts, for 2016 and 2022 ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ellaampy/SmallMinesDS)). Its Hugging Face card says **CC BY-SA 4.0**, which conflicts with the CC BY listing elsewhere ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391411842_SmallMinesDS_A_Multi-Modal_Dataset_for_Mapping_Artisanal_and_Small-Scale_Gold_Mines)). Using it to score a detector is harmless. Shipping a model or dataset derived from it may trigger share-alike, so that needs a decision before release. Barenblitt's annual conversion maps are on Mendeley ([Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/s4rfcckr39)). Tang and Werner's CC BY 4.0 global mine polygons serve as a "known mines" mask ([GEE community catalog](https://gee-community-catalog.org/projects/global-mining/)). Google's AlphaEarth annual embeddings are CC-BY 4.0, cover 2017–2025 and are mirrored outside GEE ([GEE](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/GOOGLE_SATELLITE_EMBEDDING_V1_ANNUAL)), so they make a cheap annual classifier input. Earth Genome's MIT-licensed mining detector shows the embedding-plus-classifier pattern working operationally in the Amazon ([Earth Genome](https://www.earthgenome.org/blog/a-gold-rush-worth-stopping-ai-does-its-part)).

## Static files on a cron beat hosted APIs for a solo maintainer

The compute choice should follow from how the last attempt broke. Each hosted platform carries a specific fragility. Earth Engine introduced noncommercial quotas on 27 April 2026: **150 EECU-hours a month** on Community, and 1,000 on Contributor with a billing account. Going over throttles the project rather than stopping it, and noncommercial status must be reverified every year ([GEE tiers](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/guides/noncommercial_tiers)). The bigger issue is that the terms bar publishing "Products, Content, or Software" without authorisation ([GEE Terms](https://earthengine.google.com/terms)), and nothing found settles whether derived maps on a public site count. Copernicus openEO gives 10,000 credits a month, but since March 2026 **failed jobs consume credits and free credits are not refunded** ([CDSE](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/news/2026-3-2-platform-wide-updates-openeo-credit-billing)), which turns flaky runs into lost quota. Earth Search's `sentinel-2-c1-l2a` COGs need no auth, though the repository notes some missing items ([Element84](https://github.com/Element84/earth-search)). HLS needs an Earthdata login and reads fastest from inside us-west-2 ([Earthdata](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/learn/tutorials/discovering-hls-content-cloud-storage-connection-arcgis-pro)).

The least fragile design is STAC search over COGs, processed on **public-repo GitHub Actions runners** (4 vCPU, 16 GB RAM, 6-hour jobs, 20 concurrent jobs ([GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/runners/github-hosted-runners); [limits](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/limits))). Jobs run as a matrix of idempotent per-tile, per-period tasks that skip outputs already written. Results go out as PMTiles on **Cloudflare R2**, whose free tier includes 10M reads a month and free egress ([R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)). An open archive of composites can go to Source Cooperative, which hosts openly licensed cloud-optimised data free ([Source FAQ](https://source.coop/faq)). The design rules are what keep it alive. Several STAC sources sit behind one adapter. "No new scenes" counts as a successful run. Library versions are pinned. Earth Engine, if used, is reached through Workload Identity Federation for small synchronous pulls, never as a hard dependency.

## The recommended stack, and what real time means here

| Role | Dataset | Access path | Licence | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary detector | Sentinel-1 IW RTC, VV+VH (S1C+S1D) | OPERA RTC-S1 (AWS/ASF), PC `sentinel-1-rtc` as second source, HyP3 RTC as backfill | Copernicus free and open; OPERA NASA open | Sees through cloud, sees farmland and rivers, 6-day revisit |
| Primary confirmation | Sentinel-2 L2A, 10 m | Earth Search COGs; Cloud Score+ or SCL mask | Copernicus free and open; Cloud Score+ CC-BY-4.0 | Pits, ponds, river turbidity (NDTI anomaly) |
| Off-the-shelf comparator | OPERA DIST-ALERT-HLS and DIST-ALERT-S1 | LP DAAC / ASF / AWS COGs, not the GFW query API | NASA open | Independent farmland-capable alert, sanity check on own detector |
| Forest-reserve layer | RADD | GEE asset or GNW tiles | CC BY 4.0 | Best 10 m radar alerts inside primary forest |
| Secondary radar | NISAR L-band GCOV | ASF DAAC (HDF5) | NASA open (verify) | Canopy-penetrating, wet-season robust; adopt after Q4 2026 reprocessing |
| Validation | SmallMinesDS, Barenblitt 2007–2019, Tang & Werner polygons, PALSAR-2 ScanSAR, AlphaEarth | HF, Mendeley, Zenodo, GEE / GCS | CC BY-SA, CC BY (verify), CC BY 4.0, JAXA attribution, CC-BY 4.0 | Scoring, known-mine masking, historical baseline |
| Excluded from publication | Planet NICFI | Gated GEE | Non-redistributable | Private visual checks only |

"Real time" deserves an honest definition. Data reaches the archives within hours (Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2) to three days (HLS, NISAR). A disturbance, though, is seen first by the next radar pass, up to 6 days later. One pass can raise a provisional flag, as RADD does ([WUR](https://www.wur.nl/en/research/products-services/radd-forest-disturbance-alert)). A trustworthy alert needs two or three passes, which means **12–18 days after disturbance**. Optical confirmation arrives in one to two weeks in the dry season and can take months in the rains. A weekly cron matches the radar cadence, and the public promise should be "weekly radar alerts, monthly optical maps", not "real time".

## Conclusion

The central lesson is that the previous attempt failed on engineering trust, not on data. A detector nobody had scored and an upstream nobody controlled are both avoidable: score against SmallMinesDS and DIST-ALERT before publishing anything, and read files rather than query services. The first data spike should answer, with measurements, before any production code is written. Six things to measure: how many Sentinel-1 acquisitions per 6 days, and from which relative orbits, actually reached southern Ghana since May 2026. How many Cloud Score+ clear observations each pixel gets per month across 2019–2026. Whether OPERA RTC-S1 and DIST-ALERT-S1 are produced over Ghana with acceptable lag, and whether DIST-ALERT-S1 already fires on known Pra and Offin sites. What precision and recall a rolling-window Forkuor-style detector (both signs of change) reaches against SmallMinesDS 2022 masks. Whether NISAR GCOV has usable Ghana coverage, and in which polarisations. Whether a full monthly run fits within one GitHub Actions matrix without memory failures.

Four licensing questions also need answers before launch: the exact Copernicus attribution wording, SmallMinesDS's true licence, Earth Engine's stance on published derived maps, and NISAR's licence text. If those check out, Ghana gets something it currently lacks: an open, documented, downloadable record of where the ground is being torn up, honest about lagging the diggers by a fortnight.
